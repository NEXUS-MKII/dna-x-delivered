Why this is built the way it is

You're already halfway a buyer's agent.

There's a structural problem in how mortgage brokers work with buyer's agents. You build the client relationship. The trust, the financial picture, the investment goals. You write the loan. Then you introduce that client to a buyer's agent you have no real visibility into, and you watch the ongoing advisory relationship transfer.

That isn't a referral. That's a handoff. And handoffs cost brokers more than they realise. After settlement, the relationship goes quiet. And you're back at the front lines.

The Property Acquisitions Desk is built on a different model. You don't send the client away. You add a capability to your own business. A professional property acquisition arm that operates under your client relationship, not alongside it or instead of it.

For example: Scott, a Brisbane broker, introduced his first client to the Acquisitions Desk on a Wednesday afternoon. The client's experience was simple. Pre-approval was done. Then Scott said: "We have an acquisitions team who will help you find and secure the right property." John's team ran the search. Scott wrote the loan. The client settled. Six months later, that client's colleague needed a broker. They came to Scott. Not to an external buyer's agent they barely knew.

What you give: an introduction to a pre-approved investor client. What you receive: a professional acquisition service your client will thank you for, a referral fee on settlement, and the long-term advisory relationship that stays inside your world. Not someone else's.

When to refer

Three moments that should send a client your way

These aren't edge cases. They happen in every active broker's business every month. The question is whether they produce an introduction, or get handed to someone outside your world.

1
Trigger moment

The pre-approval that has no property

Your client is approved and ready to buy. They're not sure where to look, or they're about to make a decision you suspect is wrong. For example: "I'm thinking of buying in [suburb]" or "I've found something, what do you think?" This is the moment they need professional acquisition support. And you have it.

"We have an acquisitions desk that works alongside our finance team. They handle the property search, the due diligence and the negotiation, using a data methodology most buyer's agents don't have access to. Worth a quick call to see if they can help before you commit to anything?"
2
Trigger moment

The investor who keeps mentioning it

A long-term client with equity and investment ambitions. They've mentioned property a few times. They haven't moved, because they don't know where to start, or they had a bad experience with off-the-plan. This client is yours to activate. For example: "I've been thinking about getting into the market, but I'm not sure what to do."

"You've been thinking about this for a while. The reason most people stay stuck is they don't have someone who combines the finance side and the property selection side. Our acquisitions desk does both. Would it be worth a conversation?"
3
Trigger moment

The referral that's about to leave

A client or their colleague mentions they're looking for a buyer's agent. They've already started searching externally. For example: "Do you know a good buyer's agent?" or "My colleague is looking to buy. Anyone you'd recommend?" This introduction is about to leave your network. One sentence keeps it inside.

"Actually, we have an in-house acquisitions capability now. Let me introduce you to John. He works directly with my clients and the process is a lot tighter than going to an external agent. Would that be helpful?"
Why this is different

Four things that make this partnership distinct

The client relationship stays yours

Most buyer's agent referrals transfer the ongoing advisory relationship. The Acquisitions Desk model doesn't. John presents as part of your team, your Property Acquisitions Desk, not as an external referral. The client stays anchored to you.

Data methodology, not gut feel

John uses 20 correlated data metrics. Supply pipeline, online search interest, stock on market, days on market trend. This isn't opinion-based suburb selection. It's the same methodology he has used for his own 11-property portfolio since the late 1990s.

Limited partner numbers

John limits how many brokers he partners with at any time. That isn't a marketing line. It's a real operational constraint. When you're onboarded, you have his genuine attention. Not a team member running a system.

You learn by watching

Every deal you introduce is one you can observe from the inside. Suburb selection, negotiation, settlement. If you ever decide to build buyer's agency into your own business, this is more useful preparation than any training course. You see the real process in your actual clients' situations.
