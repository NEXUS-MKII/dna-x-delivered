What this is
A scoring tool to predict whether a carousel or infographic will stop the feed scroll, earn the save, and drive the next action — vs get a quick swipe and disappear.
Scope: 6–10 slide visual assets, 1080×1350. Use this on the slide-by-slide copy before sending to design.

Core Principle
A carousel does three jobs:
Stops the scroll on slide 1 (the hook is the whole game in the feed)
Delivers ONE job per slide (no slide tries to teach two things)
Earns the save — by slide 5 the reader thinks "I want to come back to this"
Carousels aren’t pretty slideshows. They’re save-worthy mobile cheat-sheets. The visual standard is "printable" — clean grid, big type, minimal clutter, tick boxes wherever possible.

Gates
PASS (70+): publishable. Send to design.
HOLD (60–69): working draft, weak hook or save-trigger. Run upgrade pass.
KILL (<60): weak hook, multi-job slides, or no save-trigger. Rewrite from slide 1 using UMDB Phase 4 spec.

Asset Standard (must contain)
Per ADDONE 7-Slide + UMDB Phase 4 + Phase 8:
Slide 1: scroll-stopping hook (provocation, contrarian claim, specific number, or pattern interrupt)
6–10 slides total (under 6 is a graphic, over 10 is a deck)
One job per slide — name + one job + one image of the idea
At least one save-trigger (checklist, framework summary, or self-assessment with tick boxes)
"Print / Save / Share with your ops lead" micro-line on slide 1 or footer
Final slide: ONE CTA matching campaign phase
If a single slide does more than one job, score caps at 65.

Copy/Paste Prompt
I am going to give you a slide-by-slide carousel and a description of my business, audience, and offer. Score whether the right person stops scrolling, swipes through, and saves it — or swipes past. Score using the rubric below. Return PASS (70+), HOLD (60-69), or KILL (<60).

STEP 1 — SLIDE 1 + SAVE-TRIGGER (30 pts) — heaviest weight
Slide 1 pattern interrupt (0–10):
Provocation, contrarian claim, specific number, or pattern interrupt?
Or a generic title slide ("5 Ways to Improve Your Networking")?
Would the right person stop at 7am on a phone?
Slide 1 audience lock (0–10):
Does the right reader see THEMSELVES on slide 1? (Specific noun from their world.)
Or generic enough that anyone could be the audience?
Save-trigger by slide 5 (0–10):
Is there a checklist, tick box, or self-assessment somewhere in the deck?
Could the reader hand this to their ops lead and have it still make sense?

STEP 2 — STRUCTURE (25 pts)
One-job-per-slide discipline (0–15):
Does each slide deliver exactly one idea?
Could you screenshot any single slide and have it work standalone?
Are there slides where the title and body are about different things?
Story arc (0–10):
Does the carousel build to a payoff? (Hook → Problem → Framework → Stages → CTA)
Or is it a flat list with no escalation?

STEP 3 — CTA + READABILITY (25 pts)
CTA slide (0–15):
ONE clear CTA on the final slide?
Matches campaign phase (Soft early / Hard late)?
Plain-English next step ("Comment ADD-ONE", "DM CHECKLIST"), not vague ("Learn more")?
Mobile readability (0–10):
Every slide readable on phone without zoom?
Big type, breathing room, no slabs?
Max 2–5 bullets per slide?

STEP 4 — DECK DISCIPLINE (20 pts)
Slide count (0–5):
Between 6 and 10?
Micro-line present (0–5):
"Print / Save / Share with your ops lead" on slide 1 or footer?
No symmetrical triplets (0–5):
Each slide’s body breaks symmetry — varied bullet length, different shapes, broken rhythm?
Caption present (0–5):
Has the deck been written with a paired LinkedIn caption (hook + value summary + context + CTA)?

Upgrade Pass (HOLD → PASS)
Slide 1 (+5 to +10): Replace generic title with provocation, specific number, or contrarian claim. Add one specific noun from the audience world. Add the micro-line.
One-job audit (+5 to +10): Find any slide trying to do two things — split or cut the weaker job. Match titles to bodies.
Save-trigger (+5 to +10): Add a tick-box self-assessment in the middle slides. Add a framework summary slide that works standalone.
CTA (+3 to +5): If two CTAs, kill one. Replace vague CTA with plain-English next step. Match campaign phase.

Common Failure Modes
Generic title slide ("5 Tips for Better Networking")
Slide doing two jobs — title says one thing, bullets say another
Wall of text on slide 1
No save-trigger — nothing to tick, nothing to come back to
Symmetrical triplets on every slide
Vague CTA slide ("Get in touch")
More than 10 slides — should have been an article
Brand colours fighting readability

Article-coherence check
If this carousel is built off a pillar article (Build E), the article should have already passed (70+) on the Article Rubric. Carousels built off HOLD/KILL articles inherit broken spines — fix the article first.
