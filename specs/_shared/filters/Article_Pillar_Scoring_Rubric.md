Voice Layer Note
This is an asset-mechanics rubric. Voice integrity is NOT scored here — it is handled by pairing the Voice Parameter Block (Encoder Pro / Encoder Lite) at score time. Run voice gate separately. This rubric scores ONLY whether the asset works structurally as the asset type it claims to be.

What this is
A scoring tool to predict whether a pillar article will earn the save, generate clean derivatives, and produce inbound — vs read once and forgotten.
Scope: 1,200–1,800 word articles in operator essay format. Use this BEFORE building derivatives off the article — derivatives inherit the article’s spine, proof, and framework integrity.

Core Principle
A pillar article does three jobs:
Spine — one stateable claim that everything supports
Proof — 2–3 real-week vignettes that earn the abstract claims
Derivative-readiness — extractable hooks, frameworks, and CTAs already baked in
If the article passes structurally, the carousels, scripts, and posts built off it inherit the strength. If it fails structurally, fixing derivatives won’t fix the underlying issue.

Gates
PASS (70+): publishable. Run the derivative builder.
HOLD (60–69): targeted edits before derivatives. Run upgrade pass.
KILL (<60): no spine, no proof, or list-soup. Rewrite from outline (UMDB Phase 3).

Asset Standard (must contain)
Per UMDB Phase 3 + 16-min Runbook Appendix 1, a pillar article must include:
Strong hook (provocation, confession, or scene — never setup)
Spine sentence stated early — one sentence the reader leaves with
Simple framework (3–5 parts)
2–3 proof vignettes (real-week scenes, anonymised, no invented stats)
1 steelman objection (named and addressed)
1 save-worthy element (checklist, framework summary, or self-assessment)
1 CTA (matches campaign phase)
If 2 or more elements are missing, the article fails the standard. Score caps at 65 regardless of other strengths.

Copy/Paste Prompt
I am going to give you a long-form article and a description of my business, audience, and offer. Score whether this article will earn the save, generate clean derivatives, and produce inbound — or be read once and forgotten. Score using the rubric below. Return PASS (70+), HOLD (60-69), or KILL (<60).

STEP 1 — SPINE + HOOK (30 pts) — heaviest weight
Hook (first 2 lines) (0–10):
Provocation, confession, or scene? Or a setup paragraph that warms up before saying anything?
Would the right reader stop scrolling in 2 seconds?
Spine sentence (0–15):
Is there ONE stateable claim early in the article (within first 4 paragraphs)?
Could you say it back in one sentence?
Does every section trace back to it, or does the article wander?
Spine survives extraction (0–5):
Could the spine sentence be lifted out and used as a standalone post hook?

STEP 2 — FRAMEWORK + PROOF (30 pts)
Framework coherence (0–15):
Is there a simple 3–5 part framework?
Or is it a list of 12 things masquerading as a framework?
Would one slide of it still make sense in isolation?
Proof vignettes (0–15):
Are there 2–3 real-week vignettes (specific scenes, not abstract claims)?
Anonymised but specific? No invented metrics?
Or is it generic advice with no proof of presence?

STEP 3 — STEELMAN + DERIVATIVE-READINESS (25 pts)
Steelman objection (0–10):
Does the article name the strongest counter-argument honestly?
Or pretend the objection doesn’t exist?
Derivative-readiness (0–10):
Could a VA pull 10 hooks, 5 proof lines, 5 objections, and 3 CTAs from this without re-writing?
Is there a save-worthy block (checklist, self-assessment, framework summary) that works on its own?
Save-trigger (0–5):
Is there a moment by paragraph 3–4 where the reader thinks "I want to come back to this"?

STEP 4 — CTA + LENGTH (15 pts)
CTA discipline (0–10):
ONE CTA at the end? Not stacked, not buried?
Matches campaign phase (Soft early / Hard late)?
Earned by the body, not bolted on?
Length discipline (0–5):
Within 1,200–1,800 words?
Could anything be cut without losing meaning?

THEN GIVE ME
Total score out of 100
Per-step breakdown (Spine+Hook __/30, Framework+Proof __/30, Steelman+Deriv __/25, CTA+Length __/15)
PASS / HOLD / KILL
The #1 reason this article will fail to compound (specific, direct)
Rewrite my hook (first 2 lines) so the right operator stops scrolling
State the spine sentence I should anchor on (one sentence)
Identify the weakest section: Hook, Spine, Framework, Proof, or Steelman
Specify one proof vignette I should add (with structure: scene, tension, shift)
Be brutal. Diagnosis only.

My business: [DESCRIBE]
My ideal client: [DESCRIBE]
My offer: [DESCRIBE]
My article: [PASTE]

Upgrade Pass (HOLD → PASS)
Hook (+5 to +10): Rewrite first 2 lines as provocation/confession/scene. Cut anything that warms up before the point.
Spine (+5 to +10): State the spine sentence in the first 4 paragraphs in plain English. Cut sections that don’t trace back.
Proof (+5 to +10): Add one real-week vignette: scene + tension + shift. Replace one abstract claim with a specific moment.
Steelman (+3 to +5): Name the strongest counter-argument honestly. Don’t straw-man it.

Common Failure Modes
List-soup — 12 bullets pretending to be a framework
No spine — every paragraph interesting, none connect
Generic advice — could be about any business
No proof of presence — sounds like research, not lived experience
Setup paragraph as hook — warming up instead of starting cold
Stacked CTAs at end
Reads like an essay competition entry, not an operator note
