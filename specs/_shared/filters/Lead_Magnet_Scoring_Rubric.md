What this is
A scoring tool to predict whether a lead magnet earns the print/save/share decision and creates a clean sequel pull — vs gets downloaded, glanced at, and forgotten.
Scope: 1–2 page printable PDFs, checklists, mini-guides. Use this on the copy spec before sending to Gamma/Canva.

Core Principle
A lead magnet does three jobs:
Delivers the promised win in under 5 minutes of reading
Earns the print/save/share decision (operator cheat-sheet, not aspirational PDF)
Creates a sequel pull — the reader closes it knowing exactly what they want next
Useful = printed. Pretty = ignored. The test isn’t whether it looks good. The test is whether the right operator prints it on a Tuesday morning, ticks 3 boxes, hands it to their ops lead, and books a follow-up call within the week.

Gates
PASS (70+): ship to Featured / quiz result page.
HOLD (60–69): good bones, won’t earn the save yet. Run upgrade pass.
KILL (<60): aspirational, vague, or padded. Rewrite from outline using 16-min Runbook Section E.

Asset Standard (must contain)
Per 16-min Runbook Section E + Marksmen Gamma Input 2:
Plain-English title naming the specific win
"Who this is for" line filtering in the right reader
Framework or checklist (3–7 items max)
Tick boxes / self-assessment built in
At least one filled-in example or template (showing what good looks like)
Footer with the next CTA matching campaign phase
Print-ready format (1–2 pages, big type, scannable, no clutter)
If the magnet is more than 4 pages, it’s a guide. Score it differently — most operators won’t finish it. If the framework has more than 7 items, score caps at 65.

Copy/Paste Prompt
I am going to give you a lead magnet (1-page PDF, checklist, or mini-guide) and a description of my business, audience, and the action I want them to take after reading it. Score whether the right person prints, saves, or shares this — or downloads and forgets. Score using the rubric below. Return PASS (70+), HOLD (60-69), or KILL (<60).

STEP 1 — UTILITY + SEQUEL PULL (35 pts) — heaviest weight
Save-trigger density (0–15):
Clear framework (3–7 items)?
Tick boxes or self-assessment built in?
Could the reader hand this to a colleague and have it still make sense?
Specificity (0–10):
Every action specific and observable, not vague advice?
At least one example or filled-in template?
Or generic ("be more strategic", "invest in your relationships")?
Sequel pull (0–10):
Does the footer have ONE clear next step (DM keyword, book a call, take next quiz)?
Does it feel like a natural consequence of the win, or a sales jump?
Right friction level for the reader’s intent stage?

STEP 2 — TITLE + PROMISE (20 pts)
Title specificity (0–10):
Names the specific win in plain English ("ADD ONE: the 5-minute follow-up framework")?
Or aspirational/vague ("Unlock your network potential")?
Promise match (0–10):
Does the body deliver what the title promises?
How long until the reader hits the actual framework? (Should be in the first 30 seconds.)

STEP 3 — FORMAT DISCIPLINE (25 pts)
Page count (0–10):
1–2 pages? (Anything longer is a guide, not a magnet.)
Scannability (0–10):
Big type, scannable headers, breathing room?
Mobile-readable? Phone test passes?
Clutter check (0–5):
No decorative graphics fighting the content?
Brand present but not screaming?

STEP 4 — AUDIENCE LOCK (20 pts)
"Who this is for" line (0–10):
Filters in the right reader and out the wrong one?
Or assumes "everyone could benefit"?
Voice / register match (0–10):
Operator cheat-sheet register? (Plain English, short sentences, no fluff.)
Specific to the band tier (early-stage operator vs. high-intent buyer)?

Subtype Calibration — Build D LMs
ADD ONE Framework (Low band — Levels 0–3)
On-ramp magnet. Bias scoring toward Specificity (early-stage operators need observable behaviour, not theory) and Format Discipline. Tick boxes are non-negotiable.
Relationship Ladder (Mid band — Level 2–3)
Diagnostic magnet. Bias scoring toward Save-Trigger Density — the diagnostic IS the value. Self-assessment must work standalone.
Tinderbox Quickstart (High band — Level 4)
Conversion magnet. Bias scoring toward Sequel Pull and Audience Lock — reader is high-intent, magnet must earn the call without being sales-y.

Upgrade Pass (HOLD → PASS)
Title (+3 to +5): Cut aspirational language. Replace with specific win. Test: could you describe what the reader will be able to DO after reading?
Framework (+5 to +10): If more than 7 items, cut weakest 2. Add tick boxes wherever an action is described. Replace vague verbs ("focus on") with observable ones ("send within 24 hours").
Specificity (+5 to +10): Add one filled-in example or template. Replace one generic action with a specific moment.
Sequel pull (+3 to +5): Replace generic CTA with the next step in the actual journey. Add one line under the CTA.

Common Failure Modes
Aspirational title (transformation > specific win)
Pads before getting to value — framework on page 3
Framework with 12 items pretending to be a checklist
Vague advice ("be more strategic") instead of observable actions
No tick boxes — reader has nothing to DO
No examples — reader can’t see what good looks like
Generic CTA ("Get in touch")
Over-branded — graphics fighting content
